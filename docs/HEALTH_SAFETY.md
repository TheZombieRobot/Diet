# Health and Safety Design

This document sets the rules the app must obey when it touches health. It is written for you first and for the implementing model second. Every rule in section 8 becomes an automated test (see `TEST_PLAN.md`). Where a rule rests on published guidance, the source is linked. Where it rests on a judgement call, it says so.

**The app is a personal logbook and planner. It is not a medical device, does not diagnose, and does not replace your GP, a dietitian, or an addiction service.** That sentence, in plain words, appears on the first-run screen and on the "About" screen.

**Emergency and help numbers shown in the app (New Zealand):**
- Emergency: **111**
- Healthline (24/7 health advice): **0800 611 116**
- Alcohol Drug Helpline (24/7, free, confidential): **0800 787 797**, text **8681** ([Healthpoint listing](https://www.healthpoint.co.nz/mental-health-addictions/addiction/alcohol-drug-helpline/print))
- Need to Talk: **1737** (free call or text)

## 1. First-run safety notice and readiness screen

**Mandatory alcohol safety notice, shown to everyone before anything else and not skippable.** "If you drink most days or heavily at weekends, cutting down suddenly can be dangerous. Talk to your GP or the Alcohol Drug Helpline (0800 787 797, text 8681) before making a big change. Call 111 for seizures, confusion or hallucinations." The handoff requires that this protection comes **before** any reduction happens, not as a warning that appears afterwards. Basis: NZ Drug Foundation and Te Whatu Ora CADS guidance cited in section 5, and NICE guidance that people drinking heavily (CG115 uses over 15 UK units a day or an AUDIT score of 20 or more) should be offered a structured, assisted withdrawal rather than stopping alone ([NICE CG115](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg115); the handoff's CG100 reference covers hospital management of acute withdrawal, [NICE CG100](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg100)).

**Readiness questions.** Asked once, editable later in Settings. Answers change what the app allows; they are stored only on the phone. An unanswered question counts as yes. A change in Settings (an answer, or the birth year or height in the profile) that would end a running Kickstart says so and asks before it is saved; changing it back does not restart the Kickstart, and the card afterwards names the reason.

1. Are you under 18? → If yes, or if the birth year means you may be under 18: fasting, keto targets and alcohol logging are disabled; no Kickstart, no deficit (plan at the estimate, never below the floor), no projection band, no energy estimate, no weight goals; the app works as a food and exercise planner only.
2. Are you pregnant or breastfeeding? → If yes: fasting features disabled, keto carb ceiling disabled (meals still planned), weight-goal setting disabled; no Kickstart, no deficit (plan at the estimate, never below the floor), no projection band; message to discuss diet with a midwife or GP.
3. Do you take insulin, a sulfonylurea or any medication for diabetes? → If yes: low-carb eating and fasting can change how diabetes medicines act, so the message says to check with your prescriber first and explains the hypoglycaemia risk; fasting, Kickstart and carb ceilings under 50 g stay off until you confirm your prescriber has agreed. A changed answer, or "My medication or dose has changed", needs a fresh confirmation. Basis: a review in BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care notes intermittent fasting "has the potential for hypoglycemia in patients receiving antidiabetic medications such as sulfonylureas and insulin" and should be medically supervised ([BMJ Open DRC 2020](https://drc.bmj.com/content/8/1/e001248)).
4. Do you take medication that must be taken with food, or blood-pressure or cholesterol medication? → If yes: this answer does not switch fasting off (another answer still can), with a standing reminder to check timing with your pharmacist or GP; the eating schedule starts with nothing chosen, a pattern the app chose before this answer is not kept, and a 23:1 Kickstart default is never used for this person; the cholesterol section adds a "share with your GP" export.
5. Have you ever been treated for an eating disorder, or is your weight currently low? → If yes: fasting disabled, weight-goal setting disabled, keto carb ceiling disabled (meals still planned), trend shown without targets; no Kickstart, no deficit (plan at the estimate, never below the floor), no projection band. A current weight below BMI 18.5 for your height counts as a yes.
6. Has a doctor ever told you not to exercise, or do you get chest pain, dizziness or faint during activity? → If yes: exercise plans shown only after you confirm you have discussed exercise with a GP. Based on standard pre-exercise screening practice.
7. Do you drink alcohol most days, or heavily at weekends? → Used only to decide whether the "talk to a professional before changing your drinking" card is shown prominently from day one (section 5). Yes, or no answer, shows the card from day one.

The app never says "you are safe to fast/train/diet." It says "nothing you have told the app rules this out; your GP knows more."

## 2. Weight

**What it does:** daily (or less often) weight entry, 7-day rolling average line, 30/90-day view, optional goal, and a two-phase expectation: a **Kickstart** phase for the first three weeks where a fast drop is expected and normal, then a **Steady** phase.

**Owner decision (19 Oct 2026): weight loss should be high to start.** The app is built to expect and support a strong first three weeks, not to warn about them. What it still will not do is promise a number of kilograms by a date, or push intake below the safety floor.

**Kickstart phase (three weeks from the day you start it; extendable a week at a time, at most six weeks in all)**
- The weight card says: "Kickstart: a fast drop is expected now. Most of it is water and glycogen, with some fat. Typical first weeks on a carb ceiling see several kilograms go." No upper figure is promised. On later Kickstart days (extension weeks, or a Kickstart started after 21 Kickstart days were used) the card says instead that the early drop belongs to the first three Kickstart weeks.
- No rapid-loss card is shown in the first three Kickstart weeks (the first 21 Kickstart days ever, counted across every Kickstart), however fast the line falls.
- The projected trend line shown on the four-week chart is front-loaded: steep for the first two to three weeks, then flattening to the steady pace. It is drawn as a shaded band labelled "typical range, not a promise", never as a target line.
- Nutrition can run a **Kickstart deficit** (section 4): up to 30% below the estimated daily energy, with protein held at the top of the range, net carbs at the bottom of the keto range, electrolytes reminded daily, and the 5,000 kJ floor still enforced. You choose it; the app suggests it as the default when you start.
- Stop-signs during Kickstart: dizziness on standing, palpitations, cramps that electrolytes do not settle, or feeling faint at work. The card says to eat, add salt and fluids, and talk to Healthline or a GP if it continues. After a stop-sign answer, until that Kickstart's scheduled last day (even if it is ended early), no deficit is planned, no note says a fast drop is expected, and the fasting card and Today's top line time no fast to the eating window: they say it is fine to eat now, with the fasting stop-signs (section 3). The same holds for the rest of a day on which a stop-sign question was answered yes before a start.

**Steady phase (from week 4)**
- Pace guidance shown: about 0.5–1 kg a week is the pace associated with keeping weight off; the rebound after Kickstart (a kilogram or two returning when carbs or salt go up) is explained as water, not failure.
- Rapid-loss card: shown only if the rolling average falls faster than 1 kg per week for three consecutive weeks, counting Steady weeks and any Kickstart week after the first 21 Kickstart days ever: "You're still losing quickly. That's worth a check-in with your GP, especially on a low-carb plan."

**Rules**
- The long-term goal is always labelled "long-term". The four-week chart carries the line "this is a trend, not a promise". The app never states or targets a specific number of kilograms of fat loss in a number of days.
- The Kickstart and Steady phases above govern which notes and cards appear. Pace guidance in the Steady phase follows the gradual 0.5–1 kg a week figure ([CDC, archived guidance on losing weight](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090116002322mp_/http:/www.cdc.gov/nccdphp/dnpa/healthyweight/losing_weight/index.htm); the implementing model should link the current CDC page once read). Rebounds of a kilogram or two are described as normal, never as failure.
- The long-term goal cannot be set below a BMI of 18.5 for your height. The app also shows a next milestone, never more than 20% below your current weight, so the long-term goal is approached a step at a time; the milestone is worked out by the app, never entered. Plain-language reason shown.
- The rapid-loss card never appears on one of the first 21 Kickstart days ever (counted across every Kickstart); on any other day it appears only under the rule above. Judgement call based on common clinical rules of thumb; adjustable in Settings only after reading the explanation.
- A current weight below a BMI of 18.5 for your height (the lowest of the 7-day average, the latest day's average and the latest single weigh-in) applies readiness answer 5's settings (HS-35): a running Kickstart ends, and the projection band, the long-term goal and the milestone are hidden; the weight card asks for a check-in with your GP, points to readiness question 5 and says that Kickstart, the typical range and the long-term goal are switched off. Weigh-ins and the trend still show.
- The app never shows "ideal weight" or body-fat estimates.
- Daily fluctuations are explained once ("a litre of water is a kilogram") to discourage over-reaction.

## 3. Fasting

**What it does:** start/stop timer, planned window (Kickstart default 23:1, 18:00–19:00, used only while a Kickstart period runs; otherwise 22:2, 17:00–19:00; or no fasting plan), history, streak, notes for how you felt.

**Rules**
- Presets: 16:8, 18:6, 20:4, 22:2 ("5 pm snack + dinner") and **23:1 ("one meal")**, plus custom clock times.
- **Owner decision (19 Oct 2026): 23:1 for the first three weeks (Kickstart).** The app supports this as the Kickstart default. Because one meal has to carry the whole day's protein and energy, the app checks the single sitting every day: the fasting card offers the 5 pm protein snack (22:2), without calling it a broken fast, when the planned dinner (or everything planned in the one sitting) is below the protein target (the top of the protein range while the Kickstart figures apply, the bottom otherwise: a stop-sign pause or a missing rough guide reads Steady here, as on Diet and targets and the Nutrition today card), or the day's planned food energy is below the day's energy target, or the sitting would need more than about 600 g of cooked protein food. The check is strict, with no tolerance; when the day's energy target is not available, energy counts as not shown to be met and the snack is offered. The 5,000 kJ floor is used only to refuse or warn, never as the target here. Reflux: a very large single late meal is the exact pattern that aggravates symptoms, so the reflux options (earlier dinner, split meal) are offered on the day a reflux symptom is logged and the day after. On days of heavy physical work plus a training session, the pre-session check offers the lighter session or a rest day. Alcohol during the fast shows the neutral note below. The window length is **computed from the clock times**, so a 5 pm snack and a 7 pm final meal is a 2-hour eating window (22:2), not 4 hours. The longest fast the app will plan is **24 hours**.
- **The app never insists on a fast.** If the day's food is below the protein target or the day's energy target (logged so far plus the meals still planned; alcohol never counts), the fasting card says it is fine to widen the window and offers the earlier snack. Rest, food and recovery are never framed as breaking a streak.
- **Reflux-aware options.** A history of reflux or oesophagitis is common with very large late dinners. Settings offer: move the window earlier, split the main meal into snack plus dinner, and a symptom log (heartburn, night cough, regurgitation) with a prompt to see a GP if symptoms persist or worsen. The options show on a day a symptom is logged and the day after. The symptom log always carries the "see a GP" line and a line to call 111 rather than log chest pain that spreads to the arm, jaw, neck or back or comes with breathlessness, sweating or feeling faint; and when symptoms are logged on 3 or more days in 2 weeks, or are rated stronger this week than last, the log suggests seeing a GP about them. The chest-pain line and the nudge rule are judgement calls for the owner's review (checklist item 42). The app does not diagnose.
- If a running fast passes 24 hours, the timer keeps counting (logging must stay honest) but shows: "Fasts over 24 hours are outside what this app supports. Break the fast if you feel unwell, and talk to a GP before fasting this long again." No streak credit beyond 24 hours.
- **Streak.** A completed fast of 24 hours or less counts for the day it ends. A fast that runs past 24 hours, or is still running, neither adds to the streak nor ends it; a day with no completed fast ends the count quietly, with no message. Fasts less than an hour apart count as one fast for the 24-hour rule, so ending a fast and starting another cannot get round it. A Start within that hour says so first and names the time from which a new fast counts on its own (on a 23:1 day, a fast ended for dinner and the next started straight after it would otherwise join).
- **Planned fasts stop at 24 hours.** When a planned fast would run past 24 hours (after a window change, an earlier day, or the April daylight-saving night), the app plans it to end at 24 hours and says it is fine to eat by then.
- The app never recommends extended fasting, dry fasting, or fasting as a way to "compensate" for eating or drinking.
- Stop-signs listed on the fasting screen: dizziness, fainting, heart palpitations, confusion, severe weakness. "Break your fast and, if it doesn't settle, call Healthline 0800 611 116 or 111."
- Fasting is unavailable when answer 1, 2 or 5 is yes or unanswered (or the birth year means you may be under 18, or the current weight is below BMI 18.5 for the height), or when answer 3 is yes or unanswered and the prescriber's agreement is not confirmed.
- Alcohol logged during a fasting window shows a neutral note: "Alcohol on an empty stomach hits harder and affects blood sugar." No judgement, no streak penalty beyond what the user chooses.

## 4. Nutrition and cholesterol-conscious keto

**What it does:** three diet modes (keto-preferred default, moderate low-carb, balanced; switching never deletes data); net-carbohydrate ceiling in keto mode (default 30 g/day, adjustable 20–50 g), 100 g in moderate low-carb (50–130 g), none in balanced; saturated-fat budget (default about 10% of the day's planned energy); **protein target 120–140 g/day by default**, adjustable and flagged for clinician or dietitian review; fibre guide (NRV adequate intake: 30 g men, 25 g women); sodium shown; energy estimate that treats a physically active job with a high daily step count as active, not sedentary. Fasting never removes nutritional needs: the daily protein and energy targets apply whatever the window.

**Food source and confidence.** Every nutrition number carries one of three badges: **Label** (you typed it from a product label), **Database** (NZ Food Composition Database or USDA reference), or **Estimate** (a recipe computed from ingredients, or an approximation). A recipe's confidence is the weakest of its ingredients. Raw and cooked weights are never conflated; the log asks which you weighed. Recipes carry a "heart-smart" badge when saturated fat per serve is low and the main fats are unsaturated.

**Why cholesterol-conscious:** Ketogenic eating built on butter, cream, fatty meat and processed meat raises LDL cholesterol in many people, sometimes dramatically. A published case report describes extreme LDL elevation on a ketogenic diet that improved when saturated fat was reduced ([Cureus/PMC 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10436130/)). New Zealand guidance from Health NZ notes that while "some saturated fat in the diet is acceptable, eating too much (as many New Zealanders do) can be harmful for cardiovascular health" ([Health NZ, Behind the hype: low-carbohydrate high-fat diets](https://healthed.govt.nz/products/behind-the-hype-low-carbohydrate-high-fat-diets-npa267)). The Heart Foundation's reported position favours replacing dairy fat with olive oil, oily fish, avocado, nuts and seeds, and considers up to six eggs a week acceptable for people at risk (secondary sources: [Healthy Food Guide](https://www.healthyfood.com/news/new-advice-on-meat-dairy-and-eggs-for-heart-health/), [eggs.org.nz](https://eggs.org.nz/cholesterol/)). The implementing model must link the Heart Foundation's own page once it has been located and read, and must not state a Heart Foundation position beyond what that page says.

**Rules**
- Recipe scoring favours: olive oil, avocado, nuts and seeds, oily fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel, canned tuna in oil), chicken, eggs within six a week, legumes in small keto-compatible amounts, non-starchy vegetables, garden greens.
- Recipe scoring flags: butter, cream, coconut oil, bacon, salami and other processed meats, fatty cuts, cheese above about 40 g per serve. Flagged does not mean forbidden; it means the day's saturated-fat budget is consumed faster.
- The saturated-fat budget is shown as a daily bar alongside net carbs. Exceeding it changes a colour; the app never says "bad".
- A "Cholesterol check-in" card appears on day one (the handoff asks for cholesterol-conscious defaults and for a reminder to obtain actual lipid values) and again at 8 and 12 weeks: "Low-carb eating can move cholesterol in either direction. A lipid blood test before you start and again after 8–12 weeks is the only way to know. Your GP can arrange one." The app can export a summary of average daily saturated fat and net carbs for that visit.
- **Kickstart deficit (default for the first three weeks, your choice).** Planned energy up to 30% below the estimated daily expenditure, protein at the top of the range (140 g by default), net carbs at the bottom of the keto range (20 g), a daily electrolyte reminder, and alcohol energy shown in the day's total as its own part, so the deficit is honest; the floor and the meal checks count food only. A stop-sign reported in the extension check pauses the Kickstart deficit for the rest of that Kickstart, through its scheduled last day even if it is ended early, so ending it never brings a deficit back sooner. After three weeks the planner moves to a Steady deficit of about 15–20% and says why. You can extend Kickstart one week at a time; the app asks how you feel first.
- Minimum energy floor: the planner warns if a planned day falls below 5,000 kJ (about 1,200 kcal) and will not generate automatic plans below it, in either phase. For an active job the estimate will usually be far higher; the floor is a safety net, not a target. With no energy estimate there is no energy target: the planner does not fill days by itself, and meal checks treat energy as not met.
- Household dinners: your portion and its nutrition are computed separately from the total servings, so cooking for three never changes your numbers.
- Allergens entered in settings are kept on a recipe when it is edited or scaled; substitutions never silently introduce a listed allergen.
- Electrolyte reminder (sodium, potassium, magnesium) daily during Kickstart, and for the first two weeks of any keto carb-ceiling period, with food sources from the garden (silverbeet, spinach, avocado) rather than supplement advice.
- No supplement recommendations, no claims about ketosis measurements, no "fat-burning" language.
- Nutrition figures carry a footer: "Values are averages from the New Zealand Food Composition Database (or USDA where noted). Your plate will differ."

## 5. Alcohol

**What it does:** log drinks from editable presets (volume, ABV, kcal from the label; default preset 330 mL, 5%, 94 kcal, about 1.30 standard drinks), see daily and weekly cans, standard drinks and kcal, rolling trends, alcohol-free days, optional milestones entered by you or your clinician, and NZ guidance as context. Note how you felt the next day.

**Why the defaults are safety-first:** the handoff asks the app to assume that daily or heavy weekend drinking patterns carry a real possibility of dependence and withdrawal. That is why the safety notice is mandatory on first run, why the help card can be shown from day one, and why the app never generates a reduction plan. The guidance figures are shown as public context with "no alcohol use is risk-free"; they are never presented as a safe allowance or as tailored medical advice.

**Standard drink definition and maths**
- One NZ standard drink contains **10 grams of pure alcohol** ([alcohol.org.nz](https://alcohol.org.nz/help-advice/standard-drinks), [Health NZ](https://info.health.nz/mental-health/alcohol-and-drug-addiction/drinking-responsibly)).
- Standard drinks = volume in millilitres × alcohol percentage × 0.789 ÷ 1000. Example: a 330 mL beer at 5% = 1.3 standard drinks; a 150 mL glass of 13% wine = 1.5; a 30 mL spirit at 40% = 0.95.
- The drink picker uses NZ-typical serves (330 mL bottle, 440 mL can, 150 mL wine, 30 mL spirit, 500 mL cider, 250 mL RTD) with editable volume and ABV. The label's own standard-drink number can be entered directly.

**Guidance shown in the app** (from [alcohol.org.nz](https://alcohol.org.nz/help-advice/standard-drinks) and [bpac](https://bpac.org.nz/2018/alcohol.aspx)):
- Women: no more than 2 standard drinks a day and 10 a week.
- Men: no more than 3 standard drinks a day and 15 a week.
- At least 2 alcohol-free days every week.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding (readiness answer 2 yes or unanswered): no daily or weekly figures, which would read as an allowance; instead "NZ guidance: if you are pregnant, could be pregnant or are breastfeeding, the safest choice is no alcohol. Your midwife or GP can talk it through. No alcohol use is risk-free." The wording is to be checked against the official page at item 42 and confirmed by the owner at STOP 17 (full review F30).
- Some NZ summaries add "no more than 4 on a single occasion"; this is shown with a note that it is from secondary sources, until the implementing model verifies it on an official page.
- The app states that "no level of drinking is completely risk-free," which is Health NZ's framing.

**Hard rules: what the app will never do**
- It never produces a reduction schedule, taper, "cut down by one drink a day" plan, or a quit date. **Milestones you or your clinician enter are allowed** and are drawn as "your milestone", never as "target", "limit" or "allowance".
- It never converts remaining energy budget into permission to drink, and never shows "you've earned" or "calories left for drinks". Alcohol kcal are shown as a fact in the day's total, nothing more. This is your explicit requirement, and it is also the safe default: for a dependent drinker, "stopping suddenly can be dangerous if it is not managed with the help of a medical professional" ([NZ Drug Foundation](https://drugfoundation.org.nz/drugs-a-z/alcohol)), and Auckland's community alcohol and drug service notes that unsupervised withdrawal "can be life threatening" because of complications such as seizures ([Te Whatu Ora CADS, 2024](https://www.tewhatuora.govt.nz/assets/For-health-professionals/Hospital-and-specialist-services/Mental-health-and-addiction/District-and-regional-mental-health-and-addiction-services/Auckland/CADS-resources/I-A-guide-to-safe-drinking-before-your-alcohol-withdrawal-Feb-2024.pdf)).
- It never tells you to stop drinking.
- It never estimates blood-alcohol level or says anything about driving.
- It never awards streaks or badges for alcohol-free days (it counts them, neutrally, because the guidance mentions them).

**The "talk to someone" card**
Shown, without judgement, when any of these is true:
- 7 or more consecutive days with alcohol logged, or
- weekly total at or above double the guidance figure for your sex, or
- you tick "I felt shaky, sweaty, anxious or sick when I hadn't had a drink" in the next-day note, or
- readiness answer 7 is yes or unanswered (shown from day one, dismissible but returns monthly).

Card text: "If you've been drinking most days or heavily at weekends for a while and are thinking about changing that, the safest first step is a conversation, not a sudden stop. Alcohol Drug Helpline 0800 787 797 (call or text 8681), free and 24/7, or your GP. If someone is very unwell, shaking badly, confused or having a seizure, call 111." alcohol.org.nz's advice that daily drinkers should "discuss your plan on how to do this safely with a health professional" is the basis ([alcohol.org.nz, withdrawal](https://alcohol.org.nz/wellbeing/hinengaro-mental-health/withdrawal)).

## 6. Exercise: bodyweight and beach

**What it does:** two to three full-body resistance sessions a week of about 20–35 minutes, bodyweight by default with band and dumbbell variants if you have them, rest days scheduled, gradual progression, optional beach walking, beach jogging only as a progressive opt-in, a pre-session check, a session logger, and beach safety prompts. **No compulsory running** on top of a physically demanding job with a high daily step count; the app treats that work as real activity.

**Pre-session check (every session):** pain or injury, back or leg symptoms, fatigue or poor sleep, heat exposure, hydration, and a heavy physical work day (section 3). Any "yes" offers the lighter variant or rest. Rest and recovery are counted as success in the week view. The app does not diagnose injury or promise that exercise is safe. While a fast is running (and fasting is not switched off by a readiness answer), the check suggests the lighter session first and notes that eating first is fine too. After a yes, no other session starts that day, and the day's session stays the lighter one. After a Kickstart stop-sign answer in the last 14 days (section 2; a "yes" before a start counts too, and is kept as a record for that reason), or while a running fast is past 24 hours, the check offers only the lighter session or rest, names the reason, and never says that nothing rules the session out. If either begins during a session, the session becomes the lighter one from then on and says why: no harder or equipment version, no more sets of a movement already done at another version, and the rest at the easier version; a session chosen lighter stays lighter after a reload.

**Movement library (bodyweight by default; band and dumbbell variants unlock with the Settings toggles)**
Lower body: sit-to-stand or supported squat, bodyweight squat, split squat, reverse lunge, glute bridge, single-leg bridge, calf raise, step-up; light hinge (hip hinge to a box) only if pain-free.
Upper body: wall push-up, incline push-up (bench or rail), push-up, pike push-up, band row or inverted row on a sturdy rail, band pull-apart; dumbbell variants of row and press when enabled.
Core: dead bug, bird-dog, side plank, front plank, hollow hold.
Beach-specific: soft-sand walking, firm-sand brisk walk/jog near the waterline, knee-deep wading, sand sprints (later progression only), swimming between the flags.
Mobility: hip flexor stretch, calf stretch, thoracic rotations, ankle circles.

The MVP dataset (`packages/core/data/exercises.json`) plans every movement above except hollow hold, knee-deep wading, sand sprints, swimming, push-ups on the toes (kneeling push-ups are the top push-up level for now) and pike push-ups with the hands on the floor (the pike push-up stays inclined, hands on a benchtop, a bench or a chair seat). Soft-sand walking is planned as 2 to 3 minutes of softer sand within a firm-sand walk. Session C starts both planks at the kneeling version. The beach prompts still cover the water. Band and dumbbell variants are only the ones listed here: band row, band pull-apart, and dumbbell row and press. March on the spot and an easy walk are added for warm-ups and cool-downs.

**Programming rules**
- Weekly structure aims for the NZ adult guidance: at least 2.5 hours of moderate activity across the week plus two muscle-strengthening sessions ([Eating and Activity Guidelines for New Zealand Adults](https://www.health.govt.nz/publication/eating-and-activity-guidelines-new-zealand-adults); the implementing model should confirm the exact strength wording in the 2020 PDF).
- Progression is one step at a time: add reps before adding the harder version. For each movement (one exercise, all its versions together), the app never suggests more than about 10% above the last completed week, or above the last session (DOC_REVIEW C76). After a week with only lighter sessions after a yes on the check, or rest, the next week holds at the week before with no increase (a lighter session chosen after an all-no check counts like any other session, which can only lower later suggestions); after two weeks or more without the movement, it starts again from the first amount. A change of version starts at the bottom of the range, and sets already ticked in that session count toward its amount.
- Rest days are scheduled, not optional. A session starts only when the days actually trained keep at least two rest days in every 7 days in a row; the strength and jog limits also apply to what was actually done, not only to the plan.
- Sessions start with a 5-minute warm-up and end with a cool-down.
- Beach jogging (judgement call, DOC_REVIEW C51): jogging needs the owner's opt-in and four tolerated walks. A tolerated walk is a beach walk of at least 15 minutes (the walk's shortest planned length), with every check before it answered no, effort and soreness each rated 3 or less out of 5, and no pain or back or leg symptoms at the next check, on a later day; at most one walk a day counts. Jogging pauses after a jog whose effort or soreness was above 3 or not rated (a jog left unfinished on an earlier day was not rated), or whose next check showed pain or back or leg symptoms, until one more tolerated walk on a later day. A jog dated after the phone's date (its clock set back) pauses jogging too, and a walk dated after that date counts only once the date comes.

**Stop rules shown before every session**
Stop immediately and seek help for chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting or near-fainting, sudden severe headache, or pain that changes how you move. "Call 111 for chest pain that does not settle within a few minutes."

**Beach prompts (shown when the session is tagged "beach")**
- Swim only between the red and yellow flags when lifeguards are on patrol; Bay of Plenty surf beaches are usually patrolled from October to the end of March, with hours on Safeswim ([Bay of Plenty tourism water-safety page](https://bayofplentynz.com/staying-safe-in-the-bay/staying-safe-around-the-water/)). Outside patrol, wade no deeper than knee height. Rips: relax and float, raise a hand, do not fight the current.
- Local lifeguards warn that an outgoing afternoon tide with rising swell is a risky combination ([SunLive](https://www.sunlive.co.nz/news/334723-take-care-with-surf-swells.html)).
- Sun: in the warmer months, train before 10 am or after 4 pm where possible; hat, sunscreen reapplied after sweating or swimming, long sleeves. The implementing model should link the Cancer Society SunSmart page and MetService UV index once read.
- Heat and hydration: carry water; 500 mL per hour of activity in the heat is the in-app default, with electrolytes noted if also on the carb ceiling. For a beach session, and when the check says it is a hot day, the session shows at least 500 mL: 500 mL for each started hour of its longer length estimate, with "about 500 mL for each hour you are active". The day's water guide adds the same amount for each such session (item 23).
- Sand: start barefoot work on firm, wet sand; soft sand is harder on calves and Achilles. Check for glass and shells. Do not jump from dunes.
- Never swim alone, never after drinking (the app does not compute this; it just says it).

## 7. Privacy as a safety feature

- Health records are stored only on the phone, in the browser's database for the app's origin. Clearing site data deletes them, so the app reminds you to back up once a week (every 3 days while the phone has not promised to keep the app's records). A backup is recorded only when you confirm, after exporting, that you can see the file; the browser cannot tell the app whether a download really saved.
- Backup files are JSON. Locking with a password is on by default: the password (at least 10 characters) is stretched with PBKDF2-SHA-256 (600,000 iterations) into an AES-256-GCM key with WebCrypto, the file's header is protected by the same encryption so a changed file is refused, and the app decrypts and checks a locked file before offering it. A forgotten password cannot be recovered. An unlocked file can be read by anyone who opens it, and the app says so. The laptop pairing and garden photos never go into a backup.
- The only data that ever leaves the phone is the grocery list, encrypted end to end, and only when you tap "Send to laptop".
- Pairing with the laptop uses the camera: "The camera is used only while the pairing screen scans the laptop's code. The app keeps no picture and sends nothing." The phone reads the pairing code only from its own pairing screen, with a reader bundled with the app (Chrome's own reader, which on Android comes from Google Play services and was not checked, is not used unless the owner chooses it at checklist item 35); the app tells you not to scan it with the camera app or copy it, because that would put the key where other apps can read it. On the phone, the key is kept only in the app's database, in a form the app cannot read back, and never goes into a backup, web storage or a log; there is no way to type or paste it. Anyone who photographs the laptop's code could read the lists you send, and could change the list the laptop shows, until you pair again, so the laptop shows the code only while you pair.
- Nothing is sent to any AI service. If optional AI features are ever added, they require a separate, specific opt-in that names exactly what would be sent.
- Delete-all is one action in Settings, after two confirmations, and removes every record, the laptop pairing and every garden photo, every other database and cache of the app except its own files, and its local and session storage (HS-30). It cannot reach backup files you made or shared (delete them in Files, and in email or cloud storage if you sent them there), a backup password saved in Chrome (remove it in Chrome's settings), the camera permission Chrome keeps for the app after a pairing scan (remove it in Chrome's Site settings, Camera; no web page can take back a permission), or the list and pairing on the laptop (unpair there too), or a copy of the records restored in another browser, such as on a laptop (delete all there too; checks on another browser use a Guest or Incognito window, closed straight afterwards). Other tabs of the app clear their own session storage when told of the delete-all.
- No analytics, crash reporting, advertising identifiers, or third-party scripts. Fonts and icons are bundled, not fetched.
- The extension stores only the list and tick state in Chrome's extension storage.

## 9. What this document does not do

It does not evaluate whether keto, fasting or your current drinking are right for you. That conversation is between you and your GP. The app is designed so that it will never pretend otherwise.
